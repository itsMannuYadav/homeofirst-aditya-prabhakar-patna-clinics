Psoriasis is a chronic, non-contagious autoimmune inflammatory skin condition characterized by rapid epidermal cell proliferation. In a healthy individual, skin cells undergo a natural maturation cycle lasting roughly 28 to 30 days. In contrast, individuals suffering from psoriasis experience an accelerated cellular turnover rate of just 3 to 5 days. This rapid accumulation of immature skin cells leads to thick, silver-scaled, inflamed plaques that itch, crack, and bleed.

While conventional dermatological management heavily relies on topical corticosteroids, coal tar, retinoids, or biologic immunosuppressants, these treatments often act as temporary suppressants. When medication is paused, patients frequently suffer severe rebound flare-ups. **Constitutional Homeopathy offers a safe, non-steroidal, permanent approach by addressing the internal immune dysregulation that triggers psoriasis.**

## Understanding the Primary Types of Psoriasis

Different clinical presentations of psoriasis require distinct diagnostic approaches and individualised homeopathic prescribing:

### 1. Plaque Psoriasis (*Psoriasis Vulgaris*)
The most prevalent form, accounting for nearly 85% to 90% of cases. It presents as sharply demarcated red plaques covered with silvery-white keratin scales. It predominantly affects the scalp, knees, elbows, lower back, and extensor surfaces of joints.

### 2. Guttate Psoriasis
Characterized by small, drop-shaped, salmon-pink scaly lesions distributed across the trunk and limbs. Guttate psoriasis frequently develops suddenly in children and young adults following a bacterial throat infection, such as *Streptococcal pharyngitis*.

### 3. Scalp Psoriasis
Ranging from mild, dry flaking to dense, adherent silvery crusts covering the entire scalp. Scalp psoriasis can cause temporary hair thinning due to scratching and scale removal, though hair follicles typically remain intact.

### 4. Inverse (Flexural) Psoriasis
Occurs in body folds and friction areas—such as the axillae (armpits), groin, under breasts, and gluteal folds. Unlike plaque psoriasis, lesions appear as smooth, shiny, bright red inflamed patches without heavy scaling due to localized moisture and friction.

### 5. Psoriatic Arthritis
An inflammatory joint disease affecting up to 30% of psoriasis patients. It causes joint pain, morning stiffness, and swelling in finger and toe joints (*dactylitis* or sausage digits), as well as lower back pain (*spondylitis*).

## Causes, Genetic Factors, and Common Triggers

Psoriasis is fundamentally a T-cell-mediated autoimmune response. While genetic susceptibility plays a role, environmental factors and lifestyle stressors act as triggers:

1. **Emotional & Mental Stress:** Stress triggers the release of pro-inflammatory neuro-hormones (such as Substance P and Cortisol), causing immune hyper-reactivity and immediate cutaneous flare-ups.
2. **Skin Injuries (*The Koebner Phenomenon*):** Cuts, abrasions, insect bites, sunburns, or surgical incisions can trigger new psoriasis lesions precisely at the site of trauma.
3. **Upper Respiratory Infections:** Bacterial and viral infections stimulate antibody production that cross-reacts with skin antigens.
4. **Metabolic Factors & Diet:** Consuming excessive alcohol, processed sugar, red meat, and high-fat foods exacerbates systemic inflammation.
5. **Climatic Changes:** Cold, dry winter weather deprives the skin of moisture, worsening dryness, cracking, and scaling.
