## Understanding the Stages of Hair Growth

To understand hair loss, it is essential to comprehend the natural follicular lifecycle:

1. **Anagen Phase (Growth Phase):** Active hair follicle proliferation lasting 2 to 7 years. Approximately 85% to 90% of scalp hair is in this stage at any given time.
2. **Catagen Phase (Transition Phase):** A short 2 to 3 week involutional period where hair follicles shrink and detach from the dermal papilla supply.
3. **Telogen Phase (Resting Phase):** Lasting roughly 3 months, where old hair remains in the follicle while new hair begins forming underneath.
4. **Exogen Phase (Shedding Phase):** Old hair naturally falls out to make room for new growth.

## Primary Types and Causes of Excessive Hair Loss

Hair loss presents in various clinical forms based on underlying etiology:

### 1. Androgenetic Alopecia (Pattern Baldness)
The most frequent cause of hair loss in both men and women:
- **Male Pattern Hair Loss:** Driven by high sensitivity to *Dihydrotestosterone (DHT)*, leading to follicle miniaturization, receding M-shaped hairlines, and thinning at the crown.
- **Female Pattern Hair Loss:** Diffuse thinning across the mid-scalp and widening of the central part while maintaining the frontal hairline.

### 2. Telogen Effluvium
A temporary condition triggered by physical or emotional shocks—such as severe fever, Dengue/Typhoid recovery, postpartum hormonal drops, rapid weight loss, or surgical trauma. Large numbers of Anagen hairs enter the Telogen phase prematurely, resulting in sudden, diffuse shedding 2 to 3 months later.

### 3. Alopecia Areata
An autoimmune disorder where immune T-cells attack hair follicles, resulting in sudden, well-demarcated round or oval smooth bald patches on the scalp, beard, or eyebrows.

### 4. Scalp Disorders (Tinea Capitis & Seborrheic Dermatitis)
Severe dandruff, fungal scalp infections, and oily seborrhea cause localized inflammation and scalp itching, clogging hair roots and precipitating premature hair fall.
